What is this data?

The Baseline Resilience Indicators for Communities Index is a dataset constructed by the University of South Carolina to highlight six different categories of natural disaster resilience on a county level. The BRIC dataset contains six categories of 49 aggregated variables from 2010, 2015, and 2020 as of present, but this site represents only the 2020 data for Illinois. Index values range from 0 to 1, with 0 being not resilient in a category, and 1 being very resilient.

Resilience Type Summaries

Below is a brief summary of the six resilience types included in this site. More thorough descriptions are listed on the BRIC homepage, linked above. A full summary of the 49 variables involved in calculating each index and how they attribute to them can be found here, as a PDF included on the BRIC homepage.

  • Social/Cultural/Human Wellbeing: Physical attributes regarding populations and their values. Includes quality of education, transporation access, health insurance access, etc.
  • Economic/Financial: Economic assets. Includes homeownership, employment rate, income inequality, etc.
  • Infrastructure/Housing: Buildings and infrastructure. Includes structure sturdiness, availability of housing, evacuation routes, temporary shelter availability, etc.
  • Institutional/Governance: Access to resources and the ability to influence their distribution. Includes flood insurance coverage, disaster aid experience, nuclear accident planning, etc.
  • Community Capacity: Social networks and connections between individuals and communities. Includes volunteering rates, political engagement, civic organizations, etc.
  • Environmental/Natural: Natural resource use and environmental conditions. Includes food supplies, water stress, energy use, etc.

Data Source

Baseline Resilience Indicators for Communities Index from the University of South Carolina.
